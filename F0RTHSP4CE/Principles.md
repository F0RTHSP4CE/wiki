# Rules And Principles

This is the top-level public baseline for how F0 works.

It combines the cultural principles people refer to all the time with the practical public rules that come up most often. More specific pages add detail, but they should not contradict this page.

## Principles

The basic principles of the community are:

1. **Be brilliant to each other**  
   Listen to each other's needs and opinions.
2. **Do not oppress and do not bother**  
   Respect boundaries. Be ready to make compromises.
3. **Give more than you take**  
   F0 grows only if people improve it. It is not a service desk or housekeeping-backed coworking box.
4. **Financial independence**  
   Donations and money do not buy extra voting power or resident status.
5. **Do-ocracy**  
   If you want to improve something small and reversible, do it. If it affects others, coordinate first.
6. **Dying is strictly forbidden**  
   Safety comes before convenience, habit, or vibes.

## Public Rules

### People And Boundaries

- Do not insult, threaten, harass, humiliate, or corner people.
- Do not behave in ways that create a hostile environment or make people feel threatened, marginalized, or unsafe.
- If somebody asks to disengage, stop pushing the interaction and cool it down.
- If a conflict starts, prefer direct and restorative communication over public escalation theater.
- If something is getting heated, move it out of the main room or main public chat.

### Shared Space

- Pull your own weight. Clean up after yourself.
- Leave common areas workable for the next person.
- Return tools and materials to their place.
- Do not quietly occupy shared space forever with your personal mess or long-term sprawl.
- If you break something, say so promptly.

### Shared Stuff

- Ask before using anything that is not clearly shared.
- Do not touch personal projects, labeled items, private storage, or resident-only areas without permission.
- Do not move, borrow, consume, reconfigure, or take apart things casually just because they are physically present.
- Do not touch dangerous chemicals or suspicious unknown substances unless you are the right person to handle them safely.

### Safety

- If you do not understand a tool, process, setup, or chemical, stop and ask.
- Do not use dangerous tools or equipment while intoxicated.
- If somebody is too intoxicated to use the space safely, the priority is safety.
- If a task is hazardous enough that you would not want to be alone when it goes wrong, do not do it alone.
- Do not keep illegal items on the premises.
- If something is broken, unsafe, overheating, smoking, or otherwise sketchy, deal with the immediate danger first and then report it.

### Smoking, Air, And Adult Content

- If you smoke or vape, do it on the sidewalk away from the doorway.
- Do not smoke on the back patio.
- Do not normalize smoke, fumes, or intoxicants in a way that makes the space unusable for other people.
- Limit access to adult content in the space, even on personal devices.

### Guests And Hosting

- Guests need a host. "Someone is inside" does not mean the space is open for drop-in visitors.
- If you bring someone in, you are responsible for helping them understand the space and the current expectations.
- Members can host a guest only when at least one resident is already in the space.
- Public events need a resident space host, even if a member is the event lead.

### Photos And Video

- Ask before taking photos of people.
- Be careful with publication of photos showing personal belongings, project details, notebooks, or screens.
- If you capture a fast moment first, ask immediately after and delete it if someone is not okay with publication.
- Recording or streaming at events should be announced clearly.
