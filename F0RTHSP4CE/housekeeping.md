# Housekeeping

F0 works only if people do small practical things without pretending somebody else will magically clean up after them.

The short version is simple: leave it better than you found it.

## Daily Baseline

- clean up after yourself;
- return tools and materials to their place;
- do not leave tables full of your temporary chaos if you are done for the day;
- if you used the kitchen, leave the kitchen workable for the next person.

## Dishes And Mugs

- wash your own mug;
- do not leave dirty cups or dishes behind as a surprise for the next person;
- if the sink situation is getting bad, do not walk away and call that normal.

## Trash

- if bins are full, take the trash out;
- if you take the trash out, put in a new bag;
- do not leave overflowing trash as a background problem for "someone else";
- used consumables that are clearly trash should go to the trash, not to a random surface.

## Shared Surfaces

- wipe or reset work surfaces after messy work;
- do not leave soldering scraps, food mess, packaging, or sticky mystery residue in shared areas;
- if your event or project makes a room messy, cleanup is part of the activity, not an optional extra.

## After Events

After a public or semi-public event, make sure:

- tables are reset;
- dishes and cups are not abandoned;
- trash is handled;
- tools and equipment are returned;
- entrances and common routes are clear;
- the next ordinary user does not inherit your event aftermath.

## Consumables And Small Needs

If the space is running low on something practical, say so.

In practice that often means:

- add it to the internal shopping list;
- tell the relevant people instead of silently improvising forever;
- do not consume the last useful thing and then leave no trace that it needs replacing.

## Leave Better Than You Found It

You do not need to become a martyr or unpaid janitor.

But you should aim for at least one of these:

- fix the small mess you created;
- fix one extra small problem you noticed;
- avoid creating the next recurring mess.
