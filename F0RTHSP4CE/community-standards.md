# Community Standards

These standards complement, and do not replace, the narrower [chat code of conduct](chat-coc.md).

The short version is still the same:

- be brilliant to each other;
- do not oppress and do not bother;
- give more than you take;
- do not turn shared freedom into other people's problem.

## Interacting With People

- Respect boundaries, attention, and consent.
- Do not insult, threaten, harass, or corner people.
- Harassment is not magically acceptable just because it happens in direct messages instead of in public.
- Do not turn disagreement into humiliation.
- Listen when somebody tells you that a joke, topic, or behavior is landing badly.
- If you are criticizing something, try to propose a better way forward.

## Shared Stuff

- Ask before using anything that is not clearly shared.
- Owner labels, resident-only markings, and red-tape markings mean "do not touch without permission".
- Do not move, reconfigure, borrow, or consume things casually just because they are physically present.
- If you break something, say so promptly.

## Shared Space

- Leave common areas workable for the next person.
- Clean up after yourself and after your event.
- Return tools and materials to their place.
- Do not let your project quietly spill into everybody else's area forever.

## Noise And Presence

- F0 is not silent, but noise is still a shared resource.
- If there is a public event, meeting, recording, or focused work happening, adapt.
- Do not run noisy work just because you technically can.
- If you are not sure whether your activity is disruptive, ask.

## Photos And Video

- Ask before photographing people.
- Ask before publishing photos that include personal items, project details, screens, or notebooks.
- If you captured a fast moment first, ask immediately after and delete it if someone is not okay with publication.
- Event hosts should announce recording or streaming clearly in advance.

## Smoking, Substances, And Air

- Do not assume smoke, fumes, or intoxicants are acceptable just because an event feels informal.
- If something affects air quality, attention, or safety for other people, coordinate first.
- Keep the space usable for people who came to work, learn, or quietly exist.
- Do not use dangerous tools while intoxicated.
- If somebody is obviously too intoxicated to use the space safely, the priority is safety, not politeness theater.

## Children, Animals, And Guests

- If you bring somebody in, make sure they understand the space and current expectations.
- Do not bring children, animals, or extra guests into tool-heavy or crowded situations without checking first.
- If you host a guest, that responsibility does not disappear just because other people are around.

## Off-Limits Areas

As a public rule of thumb, these are off-limits unless you were clearly invited:

- private storage;
- personal project areas;
- resident-only areas;
- anything labeled as private or owner-only.

## If Something Goes Wrong

- Start with direct and respectful communication when it is safe to do so.
- If a situation is getting heated, disengage and move it out of the main room or main public chat.
- Ask a resident or event host for help if needed.
- If somebody is making the space unsafe, the priority is to stop the immediate problem, not to win the argument.

For the public escalation path, see [Conflict resolution](conflict-resolution.md).
