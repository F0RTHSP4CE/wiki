# Membership

Membership is the middle step between being a guest and being a resident.

Members are trusted regular participants who can use the space on their own, contribute at the member level, and build a real relationship with the community before deciding whether residency makes sense.

## What Membership Changes

Membership usually means:

- you can access the space for your own projects and normal use without a resident needing to be present;
- you contribute financially at the member level;
- you are trusted with more independence than a guest;
- you can take part in ongoing discussion and coordination;
- you are still not a resident, so resident-only authority stays with residents.

Membership is not only "guest plus keys". It is a trust role.

## How To Become A Member

The current practice is roughly:

1. visit first and become a known regular;
2. spend time in the space with real participation;
3. get introduced or invited into the process by an existing member or resident;
4. find at least one resident who is willing to vouch for you and take responsibility for bringing you in;
5. let residents discuss your candidacy and, when needed, formalize it through their normal approval process.

In practice, membership is not meant to be random or automatic. The public baseline is that somebody who already knows the space should want to see you here, and at least one resident should be willing to stand behind that trust.

## What Members Can Do

Members can usually:

- come to the space for their own work without needing a resident present;
- use shared resources that fit their projects and current trust level;
- participate in community discussion;
- organize projects and help with maintenance, documentation, and events;
- lead an event or activity if a resident agrees to be the space host.

## Limits Of Membership

Membership is still deliberately narrower than residency.

As a member, you should assume:

- resident-only votes stay with residents;
- the resident room is not part of default member access without resident permission;
- public events still need a resident space host;
- if you host a guest, at least one resident needs to already be in the space;
- membership does not mean unlimited authority over rooms, tools, or rules.

In practical terms, "member is not resident" usually also means:

- do not use the resident room or treat storage there as yours unless a resident gave permission;
- do not treat internal admin access, credentials, or resident-only services as part of membership;
- do not assume every tool, shelf, basement corner, or infrastructure box is public just because you can physically reach it;
- do not invite guests into the space on your own when no resident is already present;
- do not present yourself as the sole public-event space host.

If something is labeled private, looks obviously personal, is specially configured, or is clearly part of core infrastructure, ask first.

## When To Stay A Member

Membership is a good long-term fit if you want:

- regular independent access to the space;
- a lower financial commitment than residency;
- to work on projects and take part in community life without taking on the full resident role;
- time to build trust and decide whether deeper responsibility makes sense for you.

## When To Upgrade To Resident

You should look at residency if you want more than regular independent access.

Residency is the right next step when you want to:

- take ongoing responsibility for hosting, operations, and continuity;
- help make formal decisions and vote as a resident;
- host people and events with resident-level responsibility;
- support the space at the main resident contribution level;
- move from "trusted participant" to "one of the people carrying the place".

See [Residency](residency.md) for the upgrade path, timing, and current expectations.

## Financial Contribution

Members support the space at the member level.

The current public baseline is `$30` per month. The exact amount can still change with the real situation of the space.

Practical rule:

- the donation box is for casual donations and small on-the-spot support;
- regular member dues should go through `@Mike_Went`, not as an anonymous "hope somebody notices" donation.

If you need to pay your member fee, coordinate it with `@Mike_Went`.

The donation box is still useful for casual support, drinks, and small spontaneous donations, but it is not the default path for member dues.

## If You Want To Run An Event As A Member

Members can lead events, but a public event still needs a resident space host.

The practical move is:

1. decide what you want to run and what the space needs are;
2. ask a resident whether they are willing to be the space host;
3. agree who is doing announcement, scheduling, opening/closing, and cleanup before posting publicly.

If you do not know whom to ask yet, start with residents who are active in coordination and operations. Examples of reasonable first pings include `@cofob`, `@Mike_Went`, and `@sntxerror`.

Then follow [Events policy](events-policy.md).

## Quick Practical Answers

- "Where can I not go?" By default: not the resident room without resident permission, and not anywhere clearly private, specially labeled, or infrastructure-sensitive.
- "Where do I put money?" Casual support goes to the donation box; member dues go through `@Mike_Went`.
- "How do I find a resident for an event?" Ask directly and early; use known active residents instead of waiting until announcement day.
- "What internal chat should I actually watch?" `F0RTHSP4CE | pins`.

## If You Are Interested

If membership sounds right for you:

- come to events and open evenings;
- become a real, known participant instead of only asking remotely;
- talk to members and residents in person;
- ask somebody who knows you whether they are willing to introduce you or vouch for you.
