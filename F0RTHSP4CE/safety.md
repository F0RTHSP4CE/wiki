# Safety

One of F0's core principles is simple: dying is strictly forbidden.

This page is the public baseline. It does not replace common sense, tool-specific training, or event-specific safety briefings.

## General Rule

If you do not understand a tool, process, chemical, electrical setup, or risky-looking improvisation, stop and ask.

Do not guess your way through:

- mains power;
- soldering and hot tools;
- spinning, cutting, or sharp tools;
- chemical or paint fumes;
- battery charging;
- anything that can easily become a fire, smoke, or ventilation problem.

If a task is hazardous enough that you would really not want to be alone when it goes wrong, do not do it alone.

## Fire Safety

- Keep exits, corridors, and doors usable.
- Do not block escape routes with projects, boxes, or chairs.
- If something is smoking, overheating, or behaving strangely, cut power if that is safe and get people away from it.
- Hosts of workshops and public events should point out exits and fire equipment before risky activities start.

## First Aid

There is a first aid kit in the space. Recent reports place it on the shelf in the chill zone under the aquarium.

If you are running a workshop or hosting visitors:

- know where first aid is;
- know who has a phone;
- do not pretend to be a medic if you are not one.

First aid is for immediate help while professional help is on the way, not a replacement for emergency services.

## Emergency Numbers

For urgent emergency help in Georgia, call **112**.

`112` is the unified emergency number for:

- ambulance;
- police;
- fire / rescue.

If there is immediate danger to life, serious injury, fire, or another urgent emergency, call `112` first and then inform the relevant host or residents once the immediate response is underway.

## Air, Fumes, And Ventilation

Some F0 activities involve soldering, glue, paint, solvents, heated plastics, and other bad-smelling ideas.

- Use ventilation.
- If fumes are strong, stop normalizing them and fix the situation.
- Keep windows or airflow open when a process needs it.
- Do not make everybody else breathe your experiment.
- If the air is not safe to breathe, stop the activity or postpone the event.

## Equipment Safety

- Ask before using unfamiliar equipment.
- Ask before using anything that looks personal, fragile, or specially configured.
- Return tools and materials to their place.
- If you break something or notice a hazard, say so immediately.

## Guests And Public Events

Guests should not be left to guess what is safe.

If you are hosting:

- explain any non-obvious hazards;
- say which tools or areas are public-facing;
- make sure guests know what they should not touch;
- do not leave visitors unsupervised around risky setups if they do not understand the environment yet.

## If Bad Things Happened

- Handle the immediate danger first.
- Call emergency services if the situation needs professional help.
- After that, inform the relevant residents or host.
- If the problem was caused by a setup, event, or habit, document enough of it that people can prevent a repeat.
