# Hospitality

This page describes the minimum standard F0 should aim for when a newcomer arrives.

Good hospitality does not mean turning every visit into a full guided tour. It means that a new person is not left confused, ignored, or informally "owned by nobody".

## Minimum Standard

If you notice a new person in the space, do at least the following:

- say hello;
- ask whether they are new;
- ask what brought them to F0;
- make sure they know who is hosting them;
- tell them where to sit or wait and who to ask next.

## If You Are The Host

If you invited or agreed to host someone, you are responsible for making sure they understand:

- what is happening in the space today;
- which areas are public-facing and which are not;
- what tools or resources they can use;
- the basics of photos, personal belongings, and cleanup;
- how long they can reasonably stay.

If possible, also point out:

- toilets;
- kitchen basics;
- first aid;
- exits;
- guest Wi-Fi;
- who else is around.

## If You Do Not Have Time

If you are too busy to host properly, say so clearly.

Good fallback options are:

- hand the guest over explicitly to another willing host;
- offer a shorter visit instead of a vague one;
- redirect them to the next public event or open evening;
- ask them to come back another time.

Silence is worse than a direct "sorry, not today".

## No Unowned Guests

If a guest came with a host, the host should not leave them hanging without a clear handoff.

If you notice somebody staying after their host has gone, check in. The situation may be fine, but it should not remain ambiguous.

## Open Evenings

F0 benefits from having regular public-facing moments where visitors can come without complex coordination. When such evenings exist, they should be announced clearly and treated as the easiest first visit.
