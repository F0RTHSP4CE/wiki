# Events Policy

Public events are one of the main ways people encounter F0RTHSP4CE. This page is for:

- people who want to attend an event;
- people who want to propose one;
- members who want to organize or run one;
- residents who agree to host one.

## What Counts As An Event

Typical examples include:

- talks and lectures;
- workshops;
- reading groups;
- project demos;
- film nights;
- open evenings and social formats;
- practical build / repair / coworking sessions that are announced publicly.

## If You Want To Attend

- Check the public [calendar](https://f0rth.space/events.html) and the [channel](https://t.me/f0rthsp4ce).
- Read the event announcement carefully.
- Assume the event host sets the format, timing, and safety expectations for that session.
- Follow the [community standards](community-standards.md).

## If You Want To Propose An Event

The basic rule is simple: a public event needs a resident space host.

If you are not a resident:

1. Find a resident who is willing to host.
2. Explain what you want to do, how many people you expect, what space/tools you need, and whether anything is unusual.
3. Work with that host on the announcement and scheduling.

## If You Are A Member And Want To Host An Event

If you are a member but not a resident, you can still be the main organizer, speaker, or session lead.

Membership gives you space access and trust, but it does not give you resident hosting authority for public events.

In practice, that usually means:

1. find a resident who agrees to be the space host;
2. agree clearly who is doing what before the announcement goes public;
3. prepare the content, format, timing, and practical plan yourself;
4. work with the resident host on scheduling, announcement, calendar entry, and any unusual space or safety needs.

A useful split is:

- the member is the event lead;
- the resident is the space host responsible for access, opening/closing, and handling problems that affect the space.

If you are a member and want to run events repeatedly, make that explicit. Repeated trust and repeated successful events usually make coordination easier, but they still do not remove the need for clear hosting responsibility.

## Announcement And Scheduling

Normal practice is:

- make the proposal visible before the event, not after people have already made plans;
- avoid collisions with other events or space use;
- add the event to the public calendar;
- publish a public announcement in the channel.

For ordinary events, waiting at least about 24 hours before public posting is a sensible minimum. For larger, stranger, or potentially contentious events, use more lead time. In practice, a few days of visibility is much better than a same-day announcement.

## Generic Announcement Format

The examples people actually post at F0 tend to follow a simple pattern:

- title first;
- practical lines immediately after;
- then one or more short descriptive paragraphs;
- then a second language block if needed.

Use a format close to this:

```text
Event title

⏰🗓 Day, date, start-end time
📌 F0RTHSP4CE, Ana Kalandadze, 5
👉🚪 How to get to the entrance (link)
Language: EN / RU / mixed / etc.
Entrance: free / donation / ticket / materials fee / etc.
Hosts: @telegram_handle @telegram_handle

Short paragraph explaining what the event is and why somebody might want to come.

If attendance is limited, say so directly.
If people need to RSVP, prepay, or message the host first, say so directly.
If there are important constraints, say them directly.

Optional:
- what is included in the ticket or donation;
- whether people should arrive early for a tour or meet-and-greet;
- what to bring;
- whether photos / video / streaming are planned.
```

This format works well because somebody scrolling the channel can see the practical details before reading the body text.

If the event is public-facing, keep the announcement readable by somebody who has never been to F0 before.

If your event is bilingual, it is fine to post the announcement in two language blocks as long as:

- the practical details match in both blocks;
- the date and time stay identical;
- the host and payment information stay identical.

For a talk night or multi-speaker event, list each speaker in the body like this:

```text
@speaker_handle Talk title
1 short paragraph about the talk
```

Useful optional lines taken from real event posts:

- "please message me and get your ticket early";
- "maximum 8 players";
- "arrive early if you would like a tour of the hackerspace";
- "under no circumstances will any game be played for money";
- "included in your ticket is ...";
- "more will be available for a donation of ...".

## Calendar

Public-facing events should be added to the public calendar, not only posted in chat.

Keep the calendar entry simple:

- title;
- date and time;
- host;
- short description;
- link to the announcement, if there is one.

Double-check the time and make sure it does not collide with another event.

If you do not have calendar access, request it from `@cofob` on Telegram.

## What A Host Is Responsible For

At least one resident host must:

- take responsibility for the event happening in the space;
- know the timing, room use, and practical needs;
- arrive early enough to check that the space is usable;
- be present during the event, or explicitly hand hosting over;
- handle basic problems, guest questions, and disruptive behavior;
- make sure the space is cleaned up afterward;
- make sure people are not left in the space in an ambiguous way after the event.

As a rule of thumb, arriving about 15 minutes early is a good baseline.

If a member is leading the event content, the member should also:

- prepare the event description and practical requirements;
- communicate the format to guests clearly;
- stay engaged for the duration of the event unless there was an explicit handoff;
- coordinate cleanup with the resident host instead of disappearing after the interesting part is over.

## During The Event

Hosts and organizers should:

- tell people what kind of event it is;
- say whether photos, video, or streaming are happening;
- point out any unusual safety issues;
- make sure guests know what is public, what is private, and what needs permission;
- keep the event from turning into "nobody is actually responsible".

## Noise, Space Conflicts, And Cleanup

- Do not assume the whole space is yours unless that was explicitly agreed.
- If your event is noisy, messy, or room-hungry, say so when proposing it.
- Leave tables, tools, trash, dishes, and entrances in good shape after the event.
- If your format creates special waste or hazards, plan that before the event starts.

## Donations, Fees, And Commercial Framing

F0RTHSP4CE is not a paid service venue.

If you want to collect donations, a materials fee, or any money-adjacent contribution:

- clear it with the resident host in advance;
- describe it plainly;
- do not frame F0 itself as selling a service.

If the event starts looking like a commercial class, private booking, or fixed-price service, that needs explicit discussion before it is announced publicly.

## Recording And Streaming

Recording and streaming are not assumed by default.

If an event will be recorded or streamed:

- say so in the announcement;
- repeat it at the start of the event;
- give people a chance to stay out of frame or not participate in recorded parts.
