# Governance

F0RTHSP4CE is run as a horizontal community space, not as a service business.

The practical governance mix is:

- principles;
- do-ocracy for small and reversible things;
- discussion in public/internal threads;
- regular Tuesday meetings;
- resident votes when a decision needs to be formalized.

## Principles First

The cultural baseline is in [Rules and principles](Principles.md).

The important practical consequences are:

- people are expected to take initiative;
- freedom comes with responsibility;
- major changes should not surprise everybody else later;
- money does not buy extra voting power.

## Do-Ocracy

If you want to improve something small, local, and reversible, you usually do not need to wait for a grand committee blessing.

But do-ocracy is not a free pass for:

- changing shared rules without warning;
- making large or expensive changes unilaterally;
- creating a mess and calling it initiative;
- forcing everybody else to live with your experiment.

For proposals that affect other people or the space more broadly, the normal practice is to open a topic, describe the proposal clearly, and give people time to react.

## Weekly Meetings

F0 has a regular weekly meeting on Tuesday.

Typical uses:

- discuss agenda items;
- review open questions;
- interview or meet member and resident candidates;
- decide whether something should move to a vote;
- capture who is doing what next.

The useful habit is to bring topics in advance, not only at the last minute.

## Agenda And Topics

The usual workflow is:

1. create a topic or issue with enough context that somebody outside the immediate conversation can understand it;
2. wait for feedback when the topic affects others;
3. bring it to Tuesday if it needs a larger decision or coordination;
4. record the result where people can find it later.

As a baseline:

- about 24 hours is a healthy minimum for ordinary proposals;
- more consequential changes deserve more lead time;
- larger rule changes or space changes should usually reach a Tuesday meeting.

## Voting

When discussion is not enough, residents can use a non-anonymous vote.

The public-facing version of the rule is:

- residents vote;
- members are expected to join discussion and help shape proposals, but not to participate in resident-only votes;
- abstention exists as a real option;
- ordinary votes typically stay open for about one week;
- the point of a vote is to settle a question after discussion, not to replace discussion entirely.

## Roles And Decision Scope

See [Roles](Roles.md) for the compact comparison.

The short version is:

- guests can visit under the public rules;
- members are a distinct trust level: they can access the space on their own, but do not get resident-only authority;
- residents take on ongoing responsibility, pay the main space contribution, and hold the main formal decision-making power.

## What Stays Private

Some things are intentionally not fully public:

- access control details;
- internal revocation / exclusion procedures;
- private contacts and credentials;
- some infrastructure details.

Public docs explain how the community works. Internal docs carry the sensitive operational detail.
